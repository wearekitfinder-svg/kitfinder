The most collectible Chelsea kits

The 1997 FA Cup final shirt — the Umbro kit worn by Gianfranco Zola, Dennis Wise and Roberto Di Matteo when Chelsea won their first major trophy in 26 years — remains one of the most nostalgic shirts in the club's history.

Didier Drogba's Chelsea shirts from the José Mourinho and Carlo Ancelotti years are heavily collected, especially the 2004-05 title-winning season and the 2011-12 Champions League final in Munich, where Drogba scored the equaliser and the winning penalty.

The early-to-mid 1990s Umbro shirts, with their bold colour-blocked designs and short-lived shirt sponsors, are a cult favourite among collectors — a world away from Chelsea's more minimalist modern kits.

Frank Lampard's shirts from the 2004-05 and 2009-10 title-winning seasons, and Petr Čech's distinctive goalkeeper jerseys, are also popular pieces among Blues collectors.

Chelsea kits by decade

  • 1980s-90s: Umbro supplied Chelsea through this era, producing the bold, colour-blocked designs and the iconic 1997 FA Cup final shirt worn by Zola and Di Matteo.
  • 2000s: Adidas took over as kit supplier in 2006, running through the José Mourinho and Carlo Ancelotti title-winning years and Drogba's peak as a striker.
  • 2010s: The 2011-12 Champions League-winning shirt, worn in the Munich final, is the standout Adidas-era collector's piece.
  • 2020s: Nike has supplied Chelsea's kit since 2017; the 2021 Champions League final shirt from Porto, where Kai Havertz scored the winner, is the most sought-after modern jersey.
