The most collectible England kits

The 1966 World Cup Final shirt — worn by Bobby Moore, Geoff Hurst and Bobby Charlton as England beat West Germany 4-2 at Wembley for their only World Cup title — is the most historic shirt in English football. Both finalists wore Umbro that day. Original examples are extraordinarily rare.

The 1974 Admiral shirt, first worn against Czechoslovakia, was the first commercially available England shirt to carry a manufacturer's logo — a white shirt with a red and blue stripe down each arm. It kicked off the modern replica kit market and is prized by collectors of the era.

The Italia 90 Umbro shirt — its shadow-stripe pattern instantly recognisable — is tied to Gascoigne's tears and a semi-final penalty defeat to West Germany, one of English football's most emotional nights. It remains among the most requested England retro shirts.

The Euro 96 Umbro kits — the white home shirt of "Football's Coming Home" and the grey away shirt worn in the semi-final defeat to Germany on penalties — are the defining shirts of England's home tournament.

Key facts

  • England have won the FIFA World Cup once, in 1966, beating West Germany 4-2 at Wembley.
  • England have never won the UEFA European Championship, reaching the final in 2021 (lost to Italy on penalties) and 2024 (lost to Spain).
  • Admiral's 1974 deal with the FA produced the first commercially available England shirt to feature a manufacturer's logo.
  • Umbro has supplied England's kits for most of the team's history, including a 29-year spell from 1984 to 2013.
  • Nike has been England's kit supplier since 2013.
